# Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961

## Section 2 — Definition of dowry
- section: 2 | topic: dowry_definition | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: what is dowry, dowry meaning, property or valuable security, gifts, demand at marriage, mahr, dower
"Dowry" means any property or valuable security given, or agreed to be given, directly or indirectly, by one party to a marriage to the other party, or by the parents of either party or any other person to either party or any other person, at or before or at any time after the marriage, in connection with the marriage of the parties. It does not include dower or mahr in the case of persons to whom Muslim Personal Law applies.

## Section 3 — Penalty for giving or taking dowry
- section: 3 | topic: dowry_penalty | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: giving dowry, taking dowry, abetting dowry, punishment, five years, fine 15000, presents at marriage, list of presents
If a person gives, takes, or helps to give or take dowry, the punishment is imprisonment of not less than five years and a fine of not less than Rs. 15,000 or the value of the dowry, whichever is more. The court may, for adequate and special reasons recorded in the judgment, impose a lower sentence than five years. Presents given at the time of marriage without any demand are not dowry, provided a list of the presents is maintained as the rules require, giving a short description and value of each present, who gave it, and the relationship of the giver to the receiver.

## Section 4 — Penalty for demanding dowry
- section: 4 | topic: dowry_penalty | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: demanding dowry, dowry demand, husband or in-laws demand, six months, two years, fine 10000
If a person demands dowry, directly or indirectly, from the parents or other relatives or guardian of a bride or bridegroom, the punishment is imprisonment of not less than six months, which may extend to two years, and a fine up to Rs. 10,000. The court may, for adequate and special reasons recorded in the judgment, impose a lower sentence than six months.

## Section 4A — Ban on advertisements offering property in return for marriage
- section: 4A | topic: dowry_penalty | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: advertisement, share of property for marriage, offering money for marriage
Offering, through any advertisement in a newspaper, periodical, journal or by any other media, any share of property or money as consideration for a marriage is punishable with imprisonment of six months to five years, or fine up to Rs. 15,000, or both.

## Section 6 — Dowry must be handed over to the woman
- section: 6 | topic: dowry_stridhan | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: dowry belongs to wife, return of dowry, stridhan, transfer within three months, dowry kept by in-laws, trust
Where dowry is received by any person other than the woman it was meant for, that person must transfer it to the woman within three months of marriage (or within three months of receipt if received after marriage, or within three months of her turning 18 if she was a minor). Until it is transferred, the person holds it in trust for her. Failing to transfer it is punishable with imprisonment of six months to two years, or a fine of Rs. 5,000 to Rs. 10,000, or both. If the woman dies before receiving it, it passes to her heirs.

## Sections 7 and 8 — Nature of the offences and who can complain
- section: 7, 8 | topic: dowry_procedure | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: cognizable, non-bailable, non-compoundable, who can file dowry complaint, police report, Magistrate, parent or relative, welfare institution
Offences under the Act are cognizable (the police can register an FIR and investigate without a Magistrate's order), non-bailable and non-compoundable (they cannot be settled between the parties). A court takes cognizance on a police report, or on a complaint by the aggrieved person, a parent or other relative, or a recognised welfare institution or organisation. Only a Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the first class can try them.

## Sections 8A and 8B — Burden of proof and Dowry Prohibition Officers
- section: 8A, 8B | topic: dowry_procedure | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: burden of proof, Dowry Prohibition Officer, who proves dowry demand, State Government appoints
Where a person is accused of taking or demanding dowry, the burden of proving that he did not commit the offence rests on him (section 8A). The State Government appoints Dowry Prohibition Officers, who see that the Act is followed, prevent the taking or demanding of dowry, collect evidence for prosecution, and perform other duties the State assigns (section 8B). They can be assisted by an advisory board of social welfare organisations.
