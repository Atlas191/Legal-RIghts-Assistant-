# POSH Act, 2013 — provisions that complete the handbook

## Section 9 — Time limit for a complaint, and extension of the 3 months
- section: 9 | topic: complaint_time_limit | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: 3 months, three months, deadline, time limit, extension, too late, more than three months ago, complaint after three months, last incident, series of incidents, written complaint help
An aggrieved woman may make a written complaint of sexual harassment to the Internal Committee, or the Local Committee where there is no Internal Committee, within three months from the date of the incident. Where there is a series of incidents, the three months run from the date of the last incident.
- The committee may extend this time by up to three months, for reasons recorded in writing, if it is satisfied that circumstances prevented her from filing within the first three months.
- If she cannot make the complaint in writing, the Presiding Officer or any member of the committee must give her reasonable assistance to put it in writing.
- If she is unable to complain herself because of incapacity or death, another person can complain on her behalf (see handbook section 4.1).
- Extension is decided by the committee. It is not a right, and the safest course is to file within the first three months.

## Section 4(1) — Which workplaces must have an Internal Committee
- section: 4 | topic: internal_committee | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: ICC required, 10 employees, ten or more employees, which offices must have a committee, administrative units, workplace size
The employer of a workplace with ten or more employees must set up an Internal Complaints Committee in writing at the office or branch, and at every administrative unit or office located at different places or divisional or sub-divisional level. Where a workplace has fewer than ten employees, or where the complaint is against the employer, the woman goes to the Local Committee set up by the District Officer (see handbook section 3.2.1 and section 5 and 6 of the Act).

## Section 10 — Conciliation: no money settlement
- section: 10 | topic: conciliation | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: settlement, compromise, conciliation, money settlement, pay to withdraw, withdraw complaint, informal resolution
Before starting an inquiry, and at the request of the aggrieved woman, the committee may take steps to settle the matter between her and the respondent through conciliation. No monetary settlement can be made as the basis of conciliation. If a settlement is reached, the committee records it and sends it to the employer or District Officer to act on, and gives copies to both parties. Once a settlement is reached, no further inquiry is conducted. If the respondent fails to comply with the terms of the settlement, she can ask the committee to proceed with an inquiry. The handbook's rule that she, not the committee, chooses the formal route still applies.
