# BNSS 2023 — procedure for complaints, FIRs and court help

## Section 173 — Registering an FIR (including Zero FIR and e-FIR)
- section: 173 | topic: fir_registration | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: FIR, first information report, police complaint, Zero FIR, e-FIR, online complaint, any police station, police station jurisdiction, cognizable offence, free copy of FIR, formerly CrPC 154
Information about a cognizable offence can be given orally or electronically to the officer in charge of a police station. This section replaces CrPC section 154.
- Zero FIR: the police station must record the information irrespective of the area where the offence was committed. It is then transferred to the police station that has jurisdiction.
- e-FIR: if the information is given electronically, it must be signed by the informant within three days of being given, and it is then recorded.
- The informant must be given a copy of the recorded information free of cost.
- For certain offences against women, including rape offences and sexual harassment, stalking and voyeurism offences under the BNS, the information must be recorded by a woman police officer or any woman officer.
- A woman who is temporarily or permanently mentally or physically disabled can have her information recorded at her residence or a place of her choice, in the presence of an interpreter or special educator.

## Section 173(3) — Preliminary enquiry before an FIR for some offences
- section: 173(3) | topic: fir_registration | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: preliminary enquiry, police delay, 14 days, three to seven years, Deputy Superintendent of Police
For a cognizable offence punishable with three years or more but less than seven years, the officer in charge may, with the prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police, hold a preliminary enquiry within 14 days to decide whether there is a prima facie case, taking into account the nature and gravity of the offence. The FIR can also proceed straight away if there is a prima facie case.

## Section 173(4) — If the police refuse to register your FIR
- section: 173(4) | topic: police_refusal | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: police refused FIR, police not registering complaint, not taking my complaint, Superintendent of Police, SP, send complaint by post, formerly CrPC 154(3)
If the officer in charge refuses to record the information, the person can send the substance of the information in writing, by post or electronically, to the Superintendent of Police. If the Superintendent is satisfied that it discloses a cognizable offence, he must either investigate the case himself or direct an investigation by a subordinate officer.

## Section 175(3) — Application to a Magistrate to order an investigation
- section: 175(3) | topic: police_refusal | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: magistrate, court order for FIR, police inaction, affidavit, formerly CrPC 156(3), direct police to investigate
If the police do not register or investigate, a Magistrate empowered to take cognizance can order an investigation. The application is supported by an affidavit and the Magistrate may first inquire into the matter and consider the police officer's submissions. This is the court route after the police and the Superintendent of Police have both failed to act.

## Section 179 — Police cannot summon a woman to the police station for questioning
- section: 179 | topic: rights_during_investigation | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: summoned to police station, questioned at home, woman cannot be called to police station, formerly CrPC 160
Police questioning a witness may require attendance, but a woman (and a male under 15 or over 60, or a person with a mental or physical disability) cannot be required to attend any place other than the place where she resides. She can be questioned at her home.

## Section 183 — Statement before a Magistrate
- section: 183 | topic: statement_before_magistrate | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: statement to magistrate, 164 statement, recorded by judicial magistrate, woman magistrate, rape statement, formerly CrPC 164
A victim can have her statement recorded by a Judicial Magistrate during the investigation. For certain sexual offences against women, including rape, the statement is to be recorded by a Judicial Magistrate as soon as the offence is brought to the notice of the police, and by a woman Magistrate where available. A statement recorded this way carries weight in the later trial. Recording by audio-video means is permitted.

## Section 184 — Medical examination of a rape survivor
- section: 184 | topic: medical_examination | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: medical examination, rape survivor, forensic, 24 hours, consent for examination, medical report within seven days, formerly CrPC 164A
A rape survivor can be examined by a registered medical practitioner, with her consent or the consent of a person competent to give it on her behalf, within 24 hours of the police receiving the information. The doctor must prepare the report without delay and send it to the investigating officer within seven days, recording her name and address, age, description of injuries, material taken for DNA profiling, and the doctor's conclusions with reasons.

## Section 193 — Time limits for investigation of rape offences and progress updates
- section: 193 | topic: investigation_timelines | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: investigation time limit, two months, 90 days, progress update to victim, chargesheet, formerly CrPC 173
The investigation of rape offences should be completed within two months from the date the information was recorded by the officer in charge. The police must inform the informant or the victim of the progress of the investigation within 90 days, which can be done by electronic means.

## Section 366 — Trial of rape offences in camera
- section: 366 | topic: trial_privacy | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: closed court, in camera trial, privacy of victim, woman judge, formerly CrPC 327
The inquiry into and trial of rape offences is to be conducted in camera, and as far as practicable by a woman Judge or Magistrate. The proceedings cannot be printed or published without the court's permission (see BNS section 73).

## Section 396 — Victim compensation scheme
- section: 396 | topic: victim_compensation | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: compensation for victim, victim compensation scheme, rehabilitation, State Government scheme, District Legal Services Authority, formerly CrPC 357A
Every State Government, in coordination with the Central Government, must prepare a scheme to give funds for compensating victims or their dependants who have suffered loss or injury as a result of an offence and who need rehabilitation. When the court makes a recommendation, or where the offender is not traced or identified and the victim applies, the State or District Legal Services Authority decides the compensation. This is separate from any fine the court orders to be paid to the victim.

## Section 397 — Free medical treatment for victims
- section: 397 | topic: victim_medical_treatment | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: free treatment, hospital must treat, first aid, victim of rape or acid attack, private hospital, formerly CrPC 357C
All hospitals, public or private, run by the Central Government, State Government, local bodies or any other person, must provide first-aid or medical treatment free of cost to victims of rape and certain other offences against women, including acid attack under BNS section 124, and must immediately inform the police.

## Section 144 — Maintenance for wife and children
- section: 144 | topic: maintenance | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: maintenance, monthly allowance, wife unable to maintain herself, interim maintenance, child maintenance, husband not paying, formerly CrPC 125
A Magistrate of the first class can order a person with sufficient means who neglects or refuses to maintain his wife (who is unable to maintain herself), his minor child, or his parents, to pay a monthly allowance. The Magistrate can also order interim maintenance and expenses of the proceeding while the case is pending. A wife is not entitled to this allowance if she is living in adultery, refuses without sufficient reason to live with her husband, or they live separately by mutual consent. PWDVA section 20 also refers to this section.
