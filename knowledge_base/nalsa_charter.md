# Free legal aid — Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 and NALSA

## Section 12 — Who is entitled to free legal services (women are covered under clause (c))
- section: 12 | topic: free_legal_aid_eligibility | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: free lawyer, free legal aid, can't afford a lawyer, eligible for legal aid, woman or child, Section 12(c), income limit, SC ST, victim of trafficking, person in custody, disabled, disaster victim
Under section 12, the following persons who have to file or defend a case are entitled to free legal services:
- (a) a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe;
- (b) a victim of trafficking in human beings or begar (forced labour);
- (c) a woman or a child;
- (d) a person with a disability;
- (e) a victim of a mass disaster, violence, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster;
- (f) an industrial workman;
- (g) a person in custody, including in a protective home, juvenile home or psychiatric hospital;
- (h) a person whose annual income is below the amount fixed by the State Government (the ceiling differs by State).
Clause (c) means a woman does not have to prove that she is poor; being a woman is enough to qualify.

## Section 13 — Entitlement depends on a prima facie case
- section: 13 | topic: free_legal_aid_eligibility | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: prima facie case, legal aid refused, how legal aid is decided, withdraw legal aid
A person who meets the section 12 criteria is entitled to legal services if the Legal Services Authority is satisfied that she has a prima facie case to prosecute or defend. Legal aid can be withdrawn if it is found that it was obtained by misrepresentation or fraud.

## What free legal services include
- section: 2 | topic: free_legal_aid_services | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: what does legal aid cover, advocate provided free, court fee, legal advice, panel lawyer, paper book, certified copy
Legal service means rendering any service in the conduct of a case or other legal proceeding before a court, authority or tribunal, and giving advice on any legal matter. Free legal aid typically covers: a panel advocate provided at no cost, payment of court fees and other charges of the proceeding, the cost of preparing documents such as petitions and paper books, certified copies of orders and documents, and legal advice. The Constitution (Article 39A) also directs the State to provide free legal aid so that justice is not denied because of economic or other disabilities.

## Which office to approach — NALSA, State, District and Taluk authorities
- section: 3, 6, 9, 11A | topic: legal_aid_offices | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: where to apply, DLSA, District Legal Services Authority, taluka legal services committee, State Legal Services Authority, NALSA, court complex, legal aid clinic
- National Legal Services Authority (NALSA), under section 3, sets the policy and schemes at the national level.
- A State Legal Services Authority (section 6) works in each State, and a High Court Legal Services Committee (section 8A) serves the High Court.
- A District Legal Services Authority (DLSA, section 9) is in every district, usually in the district court complex. This is the office most women will approach.
- A Taluk Legal Services Committee (section 11A) serves each taluk or group of taluks.
- The Supreme Court Legal Services Committee (section 3A) serves cases in the Supreme Court.

## How to apply for free legal aid
- source: Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, as implemented by NALSA | topic: legal_aid_how_to_apply | status: supplementary, verify against local DLSA | keywords: how to apply for legal aid, application form, oral application, DLSA office, helpline 15100, panel lawyer, police station legal aid, jail legal aid
A woman can apply in writing or orally at the District or Taluk Legal Services office, usually in the court complex, and the staff help fill in the form. She can also contact the legal aid helpline (see the helplines file), or ask the Protection Officer (PWDVA section 9) or the police to connect her, since PWDVA section 5 requires them to tell her about her right to free legal services. A person in custody can ask the court or the jail legal aid clinic. Once the application is accepted, a panel lawyer is assigned to her case.

## Lok Adalat — settling disputes without a long trial
- section: 19, 21 | topic: lok_adalat | status: supplementary, verify against India Code | keywords: Lok Adalat, settlement, compromise, no court fee, final award, pre-litigation, mediation
Lok Adalats (section 19) are organised by the legal services authorities to settle disputes by compromise, including cases before they reach court. There is no court fee, and where a case already filed is settled, the court fee paid is refunded (section 21). An award made by a Lok Adalat is treated as a decree of a civil court and is final, with no appeal against it. A Lok Adalat cannot decide offences that cannot legally be compromised, so serious offences such as rape are not settled this way.
