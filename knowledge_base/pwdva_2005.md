# PWDVA 2005 — civil remedies for domestic violence

## Section 2 — Key definitions (aggrieved person, domestic relationship, shared household, respondent)
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 2 | topic: who_is_covered | keywords: who can use this law, live-in relationship, in-laws, shared household, respondent
"Aggrieved person" is any woman who is, or has been, in a domestic relationship with the respondent and alleges domestic violence by him. "Domestic relationship" is a relationship between two persons who live or have at any time lived together in a shared household, related by consanguinity, marriage, a relationship in the nature of marriage, adoption, or as family members living together as a joint family. "Respondent" is any adult male person who is or has been in a domestic relationship with the aggrieved person and against whom relief is sought; an aggrieved wife or woman in a relationship in the nature of marriage may also file a complaint against a relative of the husband or male partner. "Shared household" is a household where the aggrieved person lives or has lived in a domestic relationship, whether owned or tenanted jointly or by either of them, and includes a household of the joint family of which the respondent is a member, regardless of whether she or he has any right, title or interest in it. "Magistrate" means the Judicial Magistrate of the first class or Metropolitan Magistrate in whose area she resides (temporarily or otherwise), the respondent resides, or the violence is alleged to have happened.

## Section 3 — Definition of domestic violence
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 3 | topic: definition | keywords: what is domestic violence, physical abuse, sexual abuse, verbal abuse, emotional abuse, economic abuse, dowry demand, threats
Any act, omission, commission or conduct of the respondent is domestic violence if it: (a) harms, injures or endangers the health, safety, life, limb or well-being, mental or physical, of the aggrieved person or tends to do so, including physical, sexual, verbal and emotional, and economic abuse; (b) harasses, harms, injures or endangers her to coerce her or anyone related to her to meet an unlawful demand for dowry or other property or valuable security; (c) has the effect of threatening her or anyone related to her by conduct in (a) or (b); or (d) otherwise injures or causes harm, physical or mental, to her.
Physical abuse: any act causing bodily pain, harm or danger to life, limb or health or impairing health or development, including assault, criminal intimidation and criminal force. Sexual abuse: any conduct of a sexual nature that abuses, humiliates, degrades or otherwise violates her dignity. Verbal and emotional abuse: insults, ridicule, humiliation, name calling, especially about not having a child or a male child, and repeated threats to cause physical pain to anyone she is interested in. Economic abuse: depriving her of financial resources she is entitled to or needs (household necessities for her and her children, stridhan, jointly or separately owned property, rent for the shared household, maintenance); disposing of household effects or assets she has an interest in; and prohibiting or restricting continued access to resources or facilities she is entitled to use, including the shared household. The overall facts and circumstances of the case are considered.

## Section 4 — Who can give information, and protection for the informant
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 4 | topic: reporting | keywords: report on behalf of someone, neighbour, relative, protection officer, good faith
Any person who has reason to believe an act of domestic violence has been, is being or is likely to be committed may inform the concerned Protection Officer. No civil or criminal liability is incurred by anyone for giving such information in good faith.

## Section 5 — Duties of police, Protection Officer, service provider and Magistrate
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 5 | topic: rights_information | keywords: police must inform, free legal aid, shelter, rights of the woman, complaint to police
A police officer, Protection Officer, service provider or Magistrate who receives a complaint of domestic violence, or is present at an incident or has it reported to him, must inform the aggrieved person of: her right to apply for a protection order, monetary relief, custody order, residence order, compensation order or more than one; the services of service providers and of Protection Officers; her right to free legal services under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987; and her right to file a complaint under section 498-A of the IPC where relevant (now BNS section 85). A police officer is not relieved of the duty to act under law on information of a cognisable offence.

## Sections 6 and 7 — Shelter homes and medical facilities
- act: PWDVA 2005 | sections: 6, 7 | topic: shelter_medical | keywords: safe place to stay, shelter home, hospital, medical examination
If the aggrieved person, or a Protection Officer or service provider on her behalf, asks the person in charge of a notified shelter home for shelter, that person must provide it (section 6). A notified medical facility must likewise provide medical aid on request (section 7).

## Sections 8 and 9 — Protection Officers and their duties
- act: PWDVA 2005 | sections: 8, 9 | topic: protection_officer | keywords: protection officer, domestic incident report, who helps me file, application to magistrate
The State Government appoints Protection Officers in each district, as far as possible women (section 8). Their duties (section 9) include: assisting the Magistrate; making a domestic incident report to the Magistrate on receiving a complaint and sending copies to the police station and service providers; making an application for a protection order if she wants; ensuring she gets legal aid and the prescribed complaint form free of cost; keeping a list of service providers, shelter homes and medical facilities; providing a safe shelter home if she needs one; getting her medically examined if injured and forwarding the report; and ensuring orders for monetary relief are complied with. The Protection Officer works under the Magistrate's control and supervision.

## Section 10 — Service providers
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 10 | topic: service_providers | keywords: NGO, registered organisation, counselling, legal aid
Registered voluntary associations or companies working to protect women's rights (legal aid, medical, financial or other help) may register with the State Government as service providers. They may record a domestic incident report if the woman wants, get her medically examined, and ensure shelter in a shelter home, forwarding copies to the Magistrate, Protection Officer and police station. Service providers acting in good faith are protected from suits and prosecution.

## Section 12 — Application to the Magistrate
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 12 | topic: procedure | keywords: how to apply, court application, time limit, hearing date, 60 days, compensation
An aggrieved person, a Protection Officer or any other person on her behalf may apply to the Magistrate for one or more reliefs. Before passing an order the Magistrate must consider any domestic incident report from the Protection Officer or service provider. The relief may include compensation or damages, without prejudice to her right to sue separately (any amount paid is set off against a later decree). The Magistrate fixes the first hearing, ordinarily within three days of receiving the application, and must endeavour to dispose of the application within sixty days of the first hearing.

## Section 13 — Service of notice
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 13 | topic: procedure
The Magistrate gives notice of the hearing date to the Protection Officer, who serves it on the respondent and anyone else the Magistrate directs, within two days or such further reasonable time as the Magistrate allows.

## Section 14 — Counselling and Section 15 — Welfare expert
- act: PWDVA 2005 | sections: 14, 15 | topic: procedure
At any stage the Magistrate may direct the respondent or aggrieved person, singly or jointly, to undergo counselling with a qualified member of a service provider, and must then fix the next hearing within two months (section 14). The Magistrate may secure the help of a welfare expert, preferably a woman (section 15).

## Section 16 — Proceedings in camera
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 16 | topic: privacy | keywords: private hearing, closed court
If the circumstances warrant and either party wishes, the Magistrate may hold proceedings in camera (privately).

## Section 17 — Right to reside in the shared household
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 17 | topic: housing | keywords: thrown out of house, eviction, matrimonial home, right to stay, in-laws house, husband threw me out, in-laws locked me out, kicked out of home, made me leave the house
Every woman in a domestic relationship has the right to reside in the shared household, whether or not she has any right, title or beneficial interest in it. She cannot be evicted or excluded from it or any part of it by the respondent except in accordance with the procedure established by law.

## Section 18 — Protection orders
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 18 | topic: protection | keywords: stop him contacting me, restraining order, stay away from workplace, bank account, threats to relatives, stop him calling me, stop him messaging, stay away from me, restraining order
After hearing both sides and being prima facie satisfied that domestic violence has taken place or is likely to take place, the Magistrate may pass a protection order prohibiting the respondent from: committing any act of domestic violence; aiding or abetting it; entering her place of employment (or her child's school) or any other place she frequents; attempting to communicate with her in any form, including personal, oral, written, electronic or telephonic contact; alienating assets, operating bank lockers or bank accounts used by both or by him alone, including her stridhan or other property, without the Magistrate's leave; causing violence to her dependants, other relatives or anyone who helps her; and any other act specified in the order.

## Section 19 — Residence orders
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 19 | topic: housing | keywords: residence order, remove respondent from house, alternate accommodation, rent, police protection
While disposing of a section 12 application, if satisfied that domestic violence has taken place, the Magistrate may: restrain the respondent from dispossessing or disturbing her possession of the shared household (whether or not he has an interest in it); direct him to remove himself from the shared household; restrain him or his relatives from entering the portion where she lives; restrain him from alienating, disposing of or encumbering the shared household or renouncing his rights in it without leave; or direct him to secure alternate accommodation of the same level or pay rent for it. No order directing removal from the shared household can be passed against a woman. The Magistrate may add conditions for her or her child's safety, require a bond with or without sureties, direct the nearest police station to give protection or help implement the order, impose rent and other payment obligations, and direct return of her stridhan or other property or valuable security she is entitled to.

## Section 20 — Monetary relief
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 20 | topic: money | keywords: maintenance, expenses, medical bills, loss of earnings, salary deduction
The Magistrate may direct the respondent to pay monetary relief for expenses incurred and losses suffered by the aggrieved person and her children as a result of domestic violence, including loss of earnings, medical expenses, loss from destruction, damage or removal of property, and maintenance (including under or in addition to section 125 CrPC). The relief must be adequate, fair, reasonable and consistent with the standard of living she is accustomed to, and may be a lump sum or monthly payments. If he fails to pay, the Magistrate may direct his employer or a debtor to pay a portion of wages or debt directly to her or deposit it with the court.

## Section 21 — Custody orders
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 21 | topic: children | keywords: custody of children, visits, child safety
At any stage of hearing the Magistrate may grant temporary custody of children to the aggrieved person (or the person applying on her behalf) and specify visiting arrangements for the respondent; if a visit may harm the child's interests, the Magistrate must refuse it.

## Section 22 — Compensation orders
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 22 | topic: money | keywords: compensation, damages, mental torture, emotional distress
In addition to other reliefs, on her application the Magistrate may order the respondent to pay compensation and damages for injuries, including mental torture and emotional distress, caused by the domestic violence.

## Section 23 — Interim and ex parte orders
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 23 | topic: urgent_relief | keywords: urgent order, immediate protection, without notice, affidavit
The Magistrate may pass any interim order he considers just and proper. If the application prima facie shows that the respondent is committing, has committed or is likely to commit domestic violence, the Magistrate may grant an ex parte order (without the respondent being heard) on the aggrieved person's affidavit under section 18, 19, 20, 21 or 22.

## Sections 24 to 26 — Free copies, duration of orders, relief in other cases
- act: PWDVA 2005 | sections: 24, 25, 26 | topic: orders
Copies of orders are given free of cost to the parties, the police station and the service provider (section 24). A protection order stays in force until the aggrieved person applies for discharge; the Magistrate can alter or revoke orders on a change of circumstances with written reasons (section 25). The reliefs in sections 18 to 22 can also be sought in any civil, family or criminal court proceeding between the parties, in addition to other relief; if she obtains relief elsewhere she must tell the Magistrate (section 26).

## Section 27 — Jurisdiction
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 27 | topic: procedure | keywords: which court, where to file, move to another city
The Judicial Magistrate of the first class or Metropolitan Magistrate where the aggrieved person permanently or temporarily resides, carries on business or is employed, or where the respondent resides, carries on business or is employed, or where the cause of action arose, is competent to grant orders. Orders are enforceable throughout India.

## Section 29 — Appeal
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 29 | topic: procedure | keywords: appeal, deadline, sessions court
An appeal lies to the Court of Session within thirty days from the date the Magistrate's order is served on the aggrieved person or the respondent, whichever is later.

## Sections 31 and 32 — Penalty for breach of a protection order
- act: PWDVA 2005 | sections: 31, 32 | topic: enforcement | keywords: respondent violates order, punishment, arrest, non-bailable
Breach of a protection order or interim protection order is an offence: imprisonment up to one year, or fine up to twenty thousand rupees, or both (section 31). The Magistrate may also frame charges under section 498-A IPC (now BNS 85) or the Dowry Prohibition Act if facts disclose those offences. The offence is cognisable and non-bailable, and the court may conclude it was committed on the sole testimony of the aggrieved person (section 32).

## Section 36 — Act in addition to other laws
- act: PWDVA 2005 | section: 36 | topic: scope
The Act is in addition to, and not in derogation of, any other law in force. A woman can therefore use this civil remedy and also a criminal complaint (for example BNS section 85).
